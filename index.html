MASTER'S THESIS · RESEARCH IN PROGRESS

GRANDE-Based Multi-Drug Resistance Prediction in Mycobacterium tuberculosis Using Whole-Genome Variant Information

Development and evaluation of machine learning approaches for predicting resistance to multiple anti-tuberculosis drugs from whole-genome sequencing-derived genomic variation.

Pusan National University Department of Information Convergence Engineering 2026–2027

01 · OVERVIEW

Research Overview

Drug-resistant tuberculosis remains a major challenge for effective tuberculosis treatment. Whole-genome sequencing provides detailed information about genomic variants in Mycobacterium tuberculosis and creates opportunities for computational prediction of antimicrobial resistance.

This research investigates machine learning approaches for predicting resistance to multiple anti-tuberculosis drugs using genomic variant information derived from whole-genome sequencing data.

02 · BACKGROUND

Background & Motivation

Phenotypic drug susceptibility testing is essential for determining tuberculosis treatment options, but genomic prediction provides an opportunity to complement conventional testing with rapid computational analysis.

Many resistance prediction approaches consider individual drugs independently or represent genomic variation primarily through the presence or absence of mutations. However, resistance phenotypes across different drugs may contain related information, while genomic variants can also be represented using additional quantitative and contextual information.

This study explores how genomic representations and machine learning strategies can be designed to improve simultaneous prediction of resistance across multiple anti-tuberculosis drugs.
